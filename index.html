Shore Power Gap Atlas

Where ships emit at berth, and where shore power is missing. AFIR readiness of 31 EU ports set against at-berth CO₂ inventories for Dutch, Norwegian and Finnish ports.

Map

Squares show AFIR readiness as DNV rated it (port interviews in 2024, updated in April and May 2025): whether each port has enough shore-power connection points installed or contracted for its container, cruise and passenger ships above 5,000 GT. Circles show at-berth CO₂, sized by tonnes per year and coloured by the share that the 2030 obligation covers.

Squares, AFIR readiness (DNV rating): no segment met (21 ports) some segments met (7) all segments met (none) not concerned by AFIR (3)
Circles, at-berth CO₂ (size = kt per year): half or more inside the 2030 obligation mostly outside it

The Dutch case

The Netherlands is the one country where both layers meet: the national Emissieregistratie publishes at-berth CO₂ by municipality and ship type, and DNV assessed four Dutch ports (Terneuzen is outside the AFIR maritime mandate). Two different gaps appear.

Beyond the mandate: at-berth CO₂ across the EU

Ships under the EU MRV regulation, mostly above 5,000 GT, report the CO₂ they emit at berth in EU and EEA ports. In 2025 that came to from ships. The AFIR and FuelEU shore-power obligation covers container and passenger ships only.

Method and sources

Supply

DNV for Transport & Environment, report 2024-1792 (Table 4-1), updated with the ports in April and May 2025 and published in T&E's briefing European ports unplugged (July 2025, Annex II). Required points are estimated from 2023 AIS traffic. The 2025 update leaves every port unchanged except Venice, whose passenger-ship requirement was revised from 216 to 2 points. A surplus in one segment is not counted against a shortfall in another. Installed points above a segment's requirement are capped at that requirement, as T&E does. Source

At-berth CO₂, Netherlands

Emissieregistratie, dataset 1990-2024 (definitive), source "Zeescheepvaart stilliggend", compartment air, by municipality and ship type. Port areas group municipalities (for example Rotterdam with Schiedam, Vlaardingen and Maassluis). The national figures include auxiliary boilers and all ship sizes. Source

At-berth CO₂, Norway and Finland

Inventory by the author from open port-call registers (Kystverket Kystdatahuset; Fintraffic Digitraffic, CC BY 4.0), auxiliary engines only, method of Cunha et al. (2026). The 2030 share applies the obligation to container and passenger ships above 5,000 GT staying more than two hours, in ports above the AFIR call thresholds. Nordic At-Berth CO₂ inventory

At-berth CO₂, EU (MRV)

EMSA THETIS-MRV, 2025 publication (version of 21 July 2026), full emission reports, field "CO₂ emissions which occurred within ports under a MS jurisdiction at berth". The carbon cost counts only ships with emissions to report under the EU ETS and applies the 2025 phase-in (70% of verified CO₂; all at-berth emissions are in scope) at the 2025 average allowance price of €74 per tonne (ESMA, 2026); allowances are surrendered later, at the price of the day. The 2026 figure applies 100% of CO₂-equivalent at the same price. Offshore ships enter the ETS in 2027 and are left out of both. Partial reports (ships that changed company during the year) are excluded, as they may overlap with full reports; full reports total 150 Mt of CO₂. Container/ro-ro ships are not counted as covered by the shore-power obligation, which applies to container ships and passenger ships. Source

Limits

  • The supply layer is a May 2025 snapshot of 31 ports, not every TEN-T port; planned or tendered projects are not counted.
  • The two CO₂ sources use different methods and years; compare shapes, not decimals.
  • Port positions are approximate centroids.

Context

The EEA reports that 71 of 179 TEN-T core ports in the EU had shore-side electricity (461 berths) and 31 of 371 comprehensive ports (78 berths), without a downloadable dataset. EEA (2025)
